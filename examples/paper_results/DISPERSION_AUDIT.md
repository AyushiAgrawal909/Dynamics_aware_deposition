## Numerical dispersion levels

The root convention is `exp(lambda*t)`, so `lambda=-i*omega`.  Four levels are
reported separately.

1. **Continuum:** the unmodified Vlasov--Poisson dielectric.
2. **Principal CIC:** the represented mode with the matched CIC round-trip
   factor `S_CIC(k)^2`.
3. **Spatially discrete:** the principal grid field coupled to
   `q=k+2*pi*(ax/dx,ay/dy)` aliases, each with its actual CIC factors.
4. **Grid/time-discrete:** the spatial response plus the exact linear
   amplification equation for the explicit-midpoint weight update.  The
   velocity integral remains continuum.  Finite-marker quadrature is measured
   separately and is not hidden in this label.

For represented wavevector `d` and spatial alias `q`, the continuous response
uses

```text
epsilon = 1 + sum_q W(d)^2 S_CIC(q)^2
          * (d.q)/(d^2 q^2 vth^2)
          * sum_beams f_b [1 + zeta_b Z(zeta_b)] .
```

The time-discrete calculation is derived from the actual midpoint update, not
from an adjusted time step or a fitted rate.  Its temporal-alias form and the
finite-marker diagonal-mode determinant are both implemented in the script.

The near-marginal E2 modes cannot be compared to the continuum value while
omitting CIC: at (12,0), doing so changes the prediction from 0.14467 to
0.19450.  Spatial aliases and the time step are small corrections and do not
explain the higher measured central value.  For (12,0), aliases shift the rate
by about `-7.9e-6` and the midpoint step by a further `-6.3e-5`.

The actual finite-marker velocity samples produce broad seed-to-seed root
variation near marginality.  Their principal-mode, time-discrete mean rates
are 0.144533 (12,0), 0.239365 (11,1), and 0.119644 (12,1), with seed standard
deviations 0.015672, 0.007903, and 0.017293 respectively.  The finite-marker
mean for (12,1) lies inside its measured CI.  This calculation is a documented
diagonal-mode approximation: it omits finite-sample coupling between the real
`+k/-k` pair and spatial aliases, so it supports a quadrature explanation but
does not replace a direct eigenanalysis of the full sampled operator.

## Disputed compensated-filter cases

The recovered compensated-filter launchers specify E2 `--mode 12` and never
specify `--ky-mode 1`.  Therefore all reported compensated-filter rate ratios
are measurements of **(12,0)**, even though the manuscript calls them binding-
corner measurements.  The rate source also omits target-mode projection; this
does not create an oblique seed in the linearized model.

Using the independently measured filter transfers in the verified dispersion
calculation gives the following grid/time-discrete predictions at (12,1):

| filter | measured (12,0) ratio [95% CI] | predicted (12,1) ratio | corner status |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| p=1, n=1 | 0.91161 [0.89519,0.91875] | 0.80096 | predicted fail; no corner run |
| p=2, n=2 | 0.97287 [0.96807,0.97502] | 0.94717 | predicted pass; no corner run |
| p=2, n=4 | 0.87535 [0.85145,0.88554] | 0.73769 | predicted fail; no corner run |
| p=3, n=4 | 0.98275 [0.97972,0.98411] | 0.96811 | predicted pass; no corner run |

The transfer calculation therefore remains internally consistent and the
third-order candidate remains analytically admissible.  It is not yet an
empirically certified protected-set result.  A small projected (12,1) campaign
with the existing seeds and fit protocol is the required follow-up; it was not
launched because the audit sequence requires resolving diagnostics first.

## Unresolved issues

- Exact historical source revisions for these campaigns remain unknown; hashes
  identify the recovered copies, not the code on the compute node at run time.
- The measured central (12,0) and (12,1) rates exceed the continuum-velocity
  grid/time roots.  The recovered finite-marker samples explain a substantial
  seed spread, but the full sampled `+k/-k` eigenproblem has not been solved.
- The higher-order filter's binding-corner rate has not been measured.  It must
  remain unresolved in validation tables until the projected corner run exists.
- The calculation addresses the verified spectral chain.  It does not assign
  these roots to the separate HYPRE/finite-difference chain.
